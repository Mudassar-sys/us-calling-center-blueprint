Yes, a European company can own and use US numbers

The question that decides everything else is not where you are registered. It is whether the carrier has verified who you are and what you will call for.

US carriers sell local and toll-free numbers to companies registered outside the US. The carrier verifies your company with its own registration and business address; a US address is not part of it. New accounts can start on tight limits for numbers and outbound calls until that verification passes, so it is the first thing we start, and your number availability and calling limits are confirmed in the blueprint before any build begins.

Every outbound call is signed with an attestation level that the receiving network reads before the phone rings:

AThe carrier knows your company and your right to the number. Your calls get this once the carrier has approved your company profile and, where it asks for one, a caller identity profile linked to it, with each number assigned in your own account. Milestone 2 is signed off only when every launch number shows A on the agreed test line. BThe carrier knows you but not your right to the number shown. CThe provider knows where the call entered its network but cannot vouch for who started it. Calls handed in from abroad usually land here, and they get answered less.

The dialer sits on a US server next to its carrier connection, for call quality and so every call starts inside the US. The attestation itself comes from your verified company and numbers, not from where the server is, and your agents' location plays no part in it.

A test call proves the signature as it leaves your carrier, and we keep each test call's record as evidence. If a call later crosses an older network, the signature can be dropped and the far end sees it unsigned, and A on its own does not stop spam labels. So after go-live we watch answer rates and labels on every number, not just the test result.

The FCC has open proposals on showing verified caller names and on marking calls that originate abroad. We build on verified identity and verified numbers now, and adjust the setup when final rules are published.

What the carrier checks

  • Company name, registration number and business address
  • An authorized representative of the company
  • Identity checks on the company before high-volume calling opens
  • The numbers you will show, assigned to your verified company profile and, where required, its caller identity profile

What we do with your numbers

  • Prepare the verification pack and open every account in your company's name
  • Register each number with the free caller registry that feeds carrier spam analytics
  • Show local numbers that match the lead's area
  • Cap calls per number per day and rotate across the pool
  • Check labels every week, find the cause behind any flag, and file a correction where a label is wrong

The setup, end to end

Three places: where your agents sit, what runs in the US, and who is on the other end of the call.

Your agents

Nothing to install beyond a browser and a headset. Audio is encrypted from the agent's browser to the US server, and the phone call itself is placed from that server, not from abroad. Each agent location passes a connection and audio test before it goes live, including a reconnect and a restrictive office network; if a location needs a relay server, it is sized and priced in the blueprint. Supervisors can listen, coach in the agent's ear or take over.

In the US

One cloud server runs the dialer for ten agents, tested at the agreed dialing pace, call legs and recording load. The carrier trunk accepts only the carrier's traffic, and browser agents log in on a separate, authenticated interface. Leads are checked on arrival and again right before each dial, so an opt-out that lands while a lead waits still stops the call. If a check cannot run, the lead waits instead of being dialed.

Your data

Your company is the controller under GDPR, so every vendor is covered by a processing agreement and offshore access runs under transfer safeguards, all in place before live lead data is used. Agents see only what the call needs, and every playback of a recording is logged.

Our recommendation: VICIdial on a US cloud server

It gives you predictive dialing, ratio dialing for power and progressive pacing, preview and manual dialing, plus inbound and blended calls in one system, with no per-seat licence. Your leads, recordings and transfer logic stay on a server your company owns. The cost is administration, which our setup and managed operations cover.

If you would rather own no servers at all, Convoso is the strongest managed option, priced per seat. It is usually sold to floors larger than ten agents, so we would confirm a 10-seat account and its price in writing before you commit.

Why not Aircall or JustCall

Both are good business phone systems. Aircall has a power dialer, which works if agent-paced dialing is enough for your volume, but no predictive dialing. JustCall does offer predictive dialing, on its separate Sales Dialer plans. It would work, but it adds a seat licence on top of minutes (its standard Pro plan alone is $49 a user a month on annual billing), and your drop-rate and transfer rules would live in a sales-team tool instead of on a server you own.

    3. Which providers can issue US numbers to a European company without a US address?

      Twilio and Telnyx can both put US numbers in the name of a company registered in Europe. Twilio's business profile accepts European registration numbers and asks for your business address, with no US address required. Telnyx limits a new account to numbers from its home country until it passes its verified tier, which adds identity checks on your company and a service address. Both sign calls from numbers you own with A attestation once your company passes their checks. Twilio also needs a caller identity profile linked to your company with each number assigned to it, and we confirm the actual result on test calls in the pilot. Where they differ is dialer traffic. Twilio objects to a high volume of unanswered attempts and a low average call length. Telnyx bars predictive dialing of unsolicited calls and charges a fee on every dropped call once more than a fifth of calls are dropped before answer. We check your exact calling use case against each carrier's current terms, price any attempt or short-call fees into your cost model, and start milestone 2 only once your account is cleared. A carrier approval is not a legal sign-off on the campaign itself; your consent records and scripts still carry that.
